The structural difference

BoltzGen treats design KPIs as a separate job. It never computes design_to_target_iptm inside the training loop. It writes an eval job spec per checkpoint, that job runs the full design → inverse-fold → refold → analysis pipeline, and _sync_metrics_to_wandb pulls the results back onto the epoch axis later.

That matters for the Mol-1 port: the KPIs are not blocked by Mol-1 and Boltz-2 being unable to share an environment. BoltzGen reached the same conclusion and built a sidecar mechanism around it. Parity is a port of EvalJobSpecCallback + wandb_sync, not a rewrite of the training loop.

Notes and caveats

Checkpoint retention is the most urgent gap. At 400-step intervals the run wrote 36 GB by step 3199 and projects to roughly 225 GB over 20k steps. BoltzGen's interval is 2500.
Training is currently stopped. The last run reached step 3199 of 20000 and died at 21:14 on 21 Nov with an empty errors/ directory — a spot preemption, surfacing loudly because the Ray task is submitted with max_retries=0. Observed rate: 13.8 s/step, so 20k steps is roughly 77 hours.
Why the Mol-1 side needs a Ray task at all. An Anyscale job entrypoint runs on the head node. finetune.py calls Mol-1's trainer directly in-process, so with the GPU on a worker the run reached a real train_step and then died in the cuEquivariance kernel with libcuda.so.1: cannot open shared object file — no NVIDIA driver, because it was executing on a CPU-only instance. BoltzGen does not hit this: Ray Train places its work on workers by construction.
Unverified. The refold-KPI port, the sidecar mechanism, and the BoltzGen ↔ Mol-1 design-directory handoff have not been run. The handoff has one known mismatch: BoltzGen's UNK carries 5 atoms (N, CA, C, O, CB) while Mol-1's carries 6 (adds CG), so an adapter must strip CG and the DV06..DV13 virtual slots.
